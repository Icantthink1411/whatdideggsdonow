Where does this data come from?

Prices are sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), which aggregates Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI average price data for food items and EIA data for gas and diesel — the same boring government spreadsheets that economists argue about on the internet. Food prices update monthly. Gas and diesel update weekly.

We report national average retail prices. Your local store may be charging more, less, or a frankly insulting amount. We do not make the prices. We just witness them and make snarky remarks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is this site?

What Did Eggs Do Now? is a free weekly grocery price tracker covering staple items like eggs, milk, butter, bread, beef, bacon, coffee, and more. Prices are national averages sourced from FRED, BLS, and EIA data.

Who runs it?

This site is run independently by a person who got tired of being surprised at the grocery store checkout. Consider it a public service. You're welcome.

How accurate are the prices?

Prices reflect national average retail costs sourced from FRED, drawing on BLS CPI data for food and EIA data for fuel. Your local store will vary — sometimes a lot. We track trends, not specific store prices.

How often is it updated?

Every Sunday. Government data releases happen weekly and we publish our update the same day.

Why are egg prices so volatile?

Egg prices fluctuate due to avian flu outbreaks, feed costs, seasonal demand, and supply chain disruptions. When a major outbreak hits, millions of hens are culled, supply drops, and prices spike. This has happened multiple times since 2022 and shows no sign of becoming less dramatic.

Can I submit a tip or correction?

Yes — email us at hello@whatdideggsdonow.com. If you notice a significant discrepancy between our numbers and what you're seeing locally, we want to know.
